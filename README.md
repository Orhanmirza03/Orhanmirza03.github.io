### AI-Augmented navigation tools, (research project)

Background and Context 
The rapid deployment of offshore wind farms (OWFs) across the North Sea, Baltic Sea, and 
beyond has created structurally constrained maritime corridors in which collision risk is already 
elevated relative to open water.  
Electronic Chart Display and Information Systems (ECDIS) remain the primary 
navigational decision-support tool under IMO SOLAS Regulation V/19. However, standard 
ECDIS implementations face critical limitations in dynamic OWEA environments: 1) radar 
tracking degradation near turbine structures due to clutter and shadowing effects; 2) insufficient 
integration of probabilistic risk models with real-time sensor fusion; 3) opaque alert generation 
that hinders operator trust and appropriate response calibration; and 4) inability to learn from 
historical traffic patterns to predict vessel intentions in constrained waterways. 
Artificial intelligence offers transformative potential to address these gaps. Recent 
advances in deep learning for radar target tracking (Ghofur & Riyanto, 2025) and explainable AI 
(XAI) interfaces create opportunities for ECDIS augmentation that were previously impractical. 
Yet no published study has experimentally validated AI-integrated ECDIS specifically for 
OWEA transit scenarios, creating a critical evidence gap for regulators, operators, and 
technology developers.

### Customer Centric App for Port Services

1)	A customer initiates a service request via email inquiry  (10 inquiries per week)
a)	Needed: start time and end time (end time depends on service)
b)	Service interval unit 1hour
c)	Start time is brutto time
d)	Round always UP
e)	Starttime, how
f)	Services
i)	Vessel rent: departure time, place where staff is located and where to bring, # members, NOT needed: border regulations (customer owned), average distances (Butinge, Palanga, quay to LNG terminal); 2+1 pilot boats (7 guests+12), 2 vessels for delegations (only within port area)
ii)	Diver rent:
g)	Different vessels:
i)	Pilot vessels can leave port area
ii)	Bigger ships can’t leave an are designd to be in it.
2)	A sales representative checks availability of the services and responds with an application form 
3)	The customer completes the form and submits it with advance payment confirmation
4)	 The sales representative notifies the relevant internal department to reserve the necessary vessel 
5)	The customer coordinates directly with the vessel crew (in case of changes, but port authority doesn’t want that)
6)	 The crew fills documents about the time spent and fuel used 
7)	The sales representative consolidates the data into the internal document management system 
8)	Accounting generates an invoice based on the compiled documentation.
